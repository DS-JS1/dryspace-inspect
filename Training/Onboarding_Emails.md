# Onboarding emails — new inspectors

Copy, paste, change the names. Two emails, deliberately.

**Why two.** The first asks for one thing: install it. Nothing else competes with
that, so it actually gets done. The second lands once they have the app in front
of them and the guidance means something. Sending everything at once produces a
folder of unread attachments.

Send the second only after they confirm they are set up.

---

## Email 1 — install it

**Subject:** New site inspection app — two minutes to set up

---

Julie, Mike,

The paper site inspection form is now an app. It has been through testing and
it's ready for you both.

It is the same form you already know — same sections, same questions — just on
the iPad, with the photos attached where they belong. Two things make it worth
having:

**It works with no signal.** Everything saves to the device as you type, within
half a second. Basements, lift shafts, carparks — it makes no difference. A flat
battery or a locked screen loses nothing.

**Your photos end up where they need to be.** Once you are back in range they
upload themselves, full quality, filed against the right job.

The practical difference is less chasing. What you record on site is what the
quote gets built from, so there is a lot less ringing you three days later asking
what you saw.

**To install it — about two minutes:**

1. Open this in **Safari**. It has to be Safari, not Chrome:
   https://ds-js1.github.io/dryspace-inspect/
2. Tap the **Share** icon — the square with the arrow — then **Add to Home
   Screen**, then **Add**.
3. Open it once from the new icon while you still have signal.

From then on, use the icon rather than a browser tab. The installed version gets
protected storage; a browser tab can be cleared out from under you.

Tell me once you are set up and I will send through two cards for the vehicle.

Jamie

## Email 2 — the cards

Send after they confirm the app is installed.

**Subject:** Site inspection app — two cards for the vehicle

---

Julie, Mike,

Now you are set up, here are the two cards. Print them, laminate them if you can,
and keep one of each in the vehicle.

**Photos — read this one first.** It is the biggest change from the paper form.

The short version: take your photos in the **Camera app**, then attach them in
the form. Not through the form's own camera. Two reasons — your camera roll keeps
its own copy as a backup, and the location detail survives.

Then when you are back in signal, sign in once with your Dryspace Microsoft
account and tap **Upload now** on the home screen. Wait until it says **All
photos uploaded**.

To answer the question everyone asks first: **you cannot lose a photo by
uploading it.** The app reads every file back out of SharePoint and checks it
arrived complete before it counts it as done. Nothing leaves your device until
then.

**Handover** — for when a job passes to someone else. Less urgent on day one, but
the five steps on that card are what stop two people editing two copies of the
same job and the client being quoted from the wrong one.

If you would rather work through it than read cards, there is a short walkthrough
— about twelve minutes, works on the iPad, nothing to log into:

https://ds-js1.github.io/dryspace-inspect/Training/Training_Module.html

And I am happy to sit down with either of you and run through a live one. Ten
minutes, and probably worth more than both cards.

Jamie

## What deliberately is not in these

**The Setup & User Guide, the Handover Protocol, the presentation.** All useful,
none of it needed to get started. An inspector who has installed the app and read
one card can do a full inspection. Send the rest when someone asks, or work
through it at a toolbox meeting.

**Anything about how it was built.** They do not need to care, and the fastest way
to lose a trade audience is to explain the plumbing.

**The word "simply".** Nothing about a new system is simple to the person meeting
it for the first time.

## When the version changes

Update the version numbers in the card filenames above. Everything else stays
true — the install steps, the links and the photo workflow have not changed since
v1.3 and are not expected to.
