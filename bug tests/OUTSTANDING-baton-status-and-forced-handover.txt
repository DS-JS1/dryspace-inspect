WHY THIS TEST MATTERS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two features have never been exercised end to end against real SharePoint:

  1. The baton status columns — whether the app actually writes them, and
     whether the colours appear as intended.
  2. Forced handover — the administrator recovery path for a lost, broken or
     abandoned device.

Everything else in v1.4 has been tested on a real device. These two have not.

BEFORE YOU START
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Delete the test folders from the Site Inspections library.
   KEEP the _diagnostics folder.
   Deleted items sit in the recycle bin for 93 days (tenant default).
   Nothing on a device is affected — that storage is entirely separate.

2. Confirm the two library columns exist, named EXACTLY:
        BatonStatus
        BatonHolder
   The app writes to those names. A mismatch fails silently, by design —
   a column problem must never block a handover.

3. Paste the column formatting into BatonStatus. The JSON is recorded in
   "03_Handover and Version Control Protocol.md", section 10, item 7 -
   copy it from there rather than retyping it:
   Column header > Column settings > Format this column > Advanced mode
   (the link sits at the BOTTOM of that pane, above Save/Cancel).
   Replace everything already in the box.

   The formatting must include PURPLE for "Recovered", or step 5 below will
   show grey and look like a failure when it is not.

4. Open the beta app and confirm the footer reads:  build v1.4.0-25
   If it reads anything lower, the cache has not turned over. Use
   "Check for app update" on the home screen, or load
   .../beta/index.html?nosw=1 once and then the normal address.


THE TEST
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1 — Create and upload
  New inspection. Fill in client, address, date, inspector name AND the
  inspection number before attaching anything.
  Attach two photos. Upload them.
  EXPECT: both upload; the form's Upload button disappears when done.

Step 2 — Hand over
  Wait a minute or two with the form open, so an automatic wip/ backup is
  written. (Backups run every four minutes and on leaving the form. Step 5
  needs one to exist.)
  Hand over through SharePoint.
  EXPECT in the library:
        BatonStatus = Waiting        shown GREEN
        BatonHolder = your name
  EXPECT on the app home screen:
        the card reads HANDED OVER and sorts below any live records.

Step 3 — Take it back
  Home screen > Take over an inspection from SharePoint.
  EXPECT: the picker lists it with a green WAITING badge at top left.
  Take it over.
  EXPECT in the library:
        BatonStatus = In progress    shown AMBER
        BatonHolder = your name

Step 4 — Browse and view
  Home screen > Browse all inspections.
  EXPECT: it reports the total in the library and how many are waiting.
  Tap the inspection. It should offer:
        Ask for the baton
        Force the handover     <- administrator only; you should see this
        View it
        Open the folder in SharePoint
  Choose View it.
  EXPECT: the form opens with every field greyed out and a black banner
          reading VIEW ONLY — nothing you change here is saved.
  Close it.

Step 5 — Force the handover  ** THE ONE THAT HAS NEVER BEEN RUN **
  Browse all inspections > the same inspection > Force the handover.
  Read the confirmation, then confirm.
  EXPECT in the library:
        BatonStatus = Recovered      shown PURPLE
        BatonHolder = Jamie Stone, recovered from Jamie Stone
  Yes, both names being yours looks absurd. That is correct — you are both
  the administrator and the holder. It proves the whole path.
  EXPECT: the inspection is back on your home screen, with an audit entry
          recording that the handover was forced and where it came from.

  If it refuses with "There is nothing in this folder to recover", there is
  no wip/ backup and nothing in archive/ yet. Hand over a second time to put
  something in archive/, then retry.

Step 6 — Storage
  Check the footer figure for device storage.
  Before this work it read 139.9 MB with no inspections on the device, because
  deleted photos were leaving their bytes behind. That is fixed and a start-up
  sweep reclaims the old ones.
  EXPECT: a small figure that roughly matches what is actually on the device.
  If it is still large with nothing on the home screen, say so — it means the
  sweep is not doing its job.


IF SOMETHING FAILS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do NOT reload the app first. A reload runs start-up recovery and rewrites the
very state worth looking at.

Go straight to:
    https://ds-js1.github.io/dryspace-inspect/beta/diagnostics.html
Tap "Read the stored state", then "Copy everything", and keep it.

That page shows two readings: what was there when the page opened, before the
app booted, and what is there now. The first is the one that matters.

Also note the build from the footer. Several builds all report v1.4.0, so the
build number is the only thing that identifies which code was running.


WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT COVERED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- A device that dies BETWEEN backups loses up to four minutes of work. No
  feature fixes that; it belongs in the training.
- Photos uploaded to a record that has already been handed over are not
  referenced by the record in SharePoint (D53). The app warns and names the
  remedy — hand over again. The real fix is for a takeover to reconcile
  against the photos/ folder, and it is not built.
- Julie and Mike's permission question: whether Contribute - No Delete permits
  the archive move. Answer it by handing one inspection over TWICE and looking
  at archive/. Open since before this work started.
